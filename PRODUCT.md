# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two equally likely mobile visitors: (1) an academic or researcher who found the paper via citation, social media, or a preprint index — scanning the abstract and scorecard on their phone before deciding whether to download the PDF; (2) a finance professional or journalist checking the signal, the BibTeX, and the author credentials between meetings. Both are expert-literate, time-pressured on mobile, and will not tolerate friction. Neither needs to be sold to — they are evaluating, not browsing.

## Product Purpose

A working paper homepage for "The Ramification Index" (Wakil, 2026). Its job is to make a credible first impression, deliver the abstract and scorecard fast, enable PDF download and citation copy in one or two taps, and point to the interactive dashboard for deeper exploration. Success means a reader on their phone can absorb the thesis, verify the 6/6 scorecard claim, and cite or download the paper without ever needing to pinch-zoom or scroll horizontally.

## Brand Personality

Rigorous, amber-lit, understated. Academic authority with a developer's precision. The amber accent (#ffb347) is the single expressive element — everything else is cool-dark and restrained. Voice is confident and declarative, not persuasive. No marketing adjectives.

## Anti-references

Generic SaaS landing pages (hero metric templates, identical card grids, gradient text). Anything that looks like a startup pitch deck. Academic pages that are just unstyled HTML. Crypto-dark neon aesthetics. Anything that would embarrass a researcher citing this in a footnote.

## Design Principles

1. **Data leads, chrome follows.** Every decorative element earns its place by directing attention to a number or a finding — never for its own sake.
2. **Expertise assumed.** No hand-holding copy, no intros that repeat the title. Readers are peers.
3. **Portable authority.** The page must look credible as a citation destination — citable, quotable, institutionally grounded.
4. **Mobile is a reading surface, not a browsing surface.** On small screens, optimize for linear reading and one-tap actions. Hide exploration tools (dashboard, full scorecard) behind explicit "open" affordances rather than forcing them into the flow.
5. **Restraint is the brand.** One accent color, no gradients on text, no borders as decoration. Confidence through precision.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. System font stack already in use — no web font load penalty. Reduced-motion: animations are minor (progress bar scale, transcript expand) — acceptable without `prefers-reduced-motion` guard, but worth adding. Touch targets on mobile should be minimum 44px.
